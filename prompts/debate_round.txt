================================================================================
Debate Rounds 1-3  -  Per-Member Debate Prompt
================================================================================

Rebuilt from scratch for each council member at each debate round. This is
single-turn prompt injection, not a multi-turn chat: the model is handed one
flat prompt containing its own previous answer, its colleagues' previous
answers, and the running vote tally. No conversation history object is passed
to the model.

Placeholders
------------
  {QUESTION_TEXT}         the case description (constant across rounds)
  {CONTEXT_BLOCK}         "" except for PubMedQA-with-context, where it is
                          literally "\n\nRELEVANT CONTEXT:\n<abstract>\n"
  {OPTIONS_STR}           options, one per line, sorted, "<letter>: <text>"
  {VALID_LETTERS}         option letters, sorted, comma-separated
  {OWN_PREVIOUS_LABEL}    "Initial" at Round 1; otherwise the previous round number
  {OWN_PREVIOUS_*}        this member's own previous selected option, confidence,
                          clinical reasoning and critical evidence
  {SUMMARY_BLOCK}         present from Round 2 onward only (format below)
  {COLLEAGUES_BLOCK}      the other four members' previous-round responses,
                          relabelled "Panelist A" through "Panelist D"
  {VOTE_DIST}             the previous round's vote distribution, rendered as a
                          Python dict literal, e.g. {'D': 2, 'B': 1, 'A': 2}

The row of "=" characters shown below as a separator is, in the actual prompt,
a run of 55 U+2550 (BOX DRAWINGS DOUBLE HORIZONTAL) characters.

================================================================================
PROMPT (verbatim template)
================================================================================

One annotation has been added that is NOT part of the prompt: the
"<- Rounds 2 and 3 only" marker on the SUMMARY OF PRIOR DISCUSSION ROUNDS line.
Everything else below is the template exactly as assembled.

You are a medical expert on a clinical consultation panel. You are participating in a structured debate with your colleagues to reach the best clinical decision.

CLINICAL CASE:
{QUESTION_TEXT}{CONTEXT_BLOCK}

OPTIONS:
{OPTIONS_STR}

=======================================================
YOUR PREVIOUS POSITION (Round {OWN_PREVIOUS_LABEL}):
  You selected: {OWN_PREVIOUS_OPTION}
  Your confidence: {OWN_PREVIOUS_CONF}
  Your reasoning: {OWN_PREVIOUS_REASON}
  Your key evidence: {OWN_PREVIOUS_EVIDENCE}
=======================================================

SUMMARY OF PRIOR DISCUSSION ROUNDS:                    <- Rounds 2 and 3 only
{SUMMARY_BLOCK}

COLLEAGUE RESPONSES FROM PREVIOUS ROUND:

--- Panelist A ---
Selected: {A_selected}
Confidence: {A_confidence}
Reasoning: {A_reasoning}
Key Evidence: {A_evidence}

--- Panelist B ---
Selected: {B_selected}
Confidence: {B_confidence}
Reasoning: {B_reasoning}
Key Evidence: {B_evidence}

--- Panelist C ---
Selected: {C_selected}
Confidence: {C_confidence}
Reasoning: {C_reasoning}
Key Evidence: {C_evidence}

--- Panelist D ---
Selected: {D_selected}
Confidence: {D_confidence}
Reasoning: {D_reasoning}
Key Evidence: {D_evidence}

CURRENT VOTE DISTRIBUTION: {VOTE_DIST}

=======================================================
INSTRUCTIONS:
Review your colleagues' reasoning carefully and critically.

Consider:
1. Do any colleagues present clinical evidence you missed or overlooked?
2. Are there flaws in your own reasoning that their arguments expose?
3. Are there flaws in THEIR reasoning that you can identify?
4. Does the clinical evidence more strongly support one answer over others?

You MAY change your position if genuinely persuaded by clinical evidence.
Do NOT simply agree with the majority without strong clinical justification.
Maintain your position if you believe your evidence is stronger.

Respond ONLY with a valid JSON object (no extra text before or after):
{
  "updated_selected_option": "<{VALID_LETTERS}>",
  "changed_position": true or false,
  "confidence": <0.0 to 1.0>,
  "reasoning_update": "<explain what changed your mind OR why you hold firm, 2-4 sentences>",
  "critical_evidence": "<the single strongest clinical evidence for your current position>",
  "counter_argument": "<1-2 sentence rebuttal to the strongest opposing argument>"
}
=======================================================

================================================================================
SUMMARY_BLOCK format (Rounds 2 and 3)
================================================================================

Deterministic text extraction, not an LLM summary. One line per debate round
completed so far:

  Round <n>: Votes=<vote dict>, Agreement=<pct>, Position changes: <changes>

The summary covers EVERY completed round, including the most recent one, which
is therefore described here and also shown in full in the colleague block below
it. At Round 2 the block holds one line (Round 1); at Round 3 it holds two
(Rounds 1 and 2).

Position changes name the model KEY, not the display name, and use a Unicode
arrow U+2192:

  Round 1: Votes={'D': 2, 'B': 1, 'A': 2}, Agreement=40%, Position changes: medgemma_4b changed C→D; rnj1_8b changed D→B

When a round produced no changes the list reads "none".

================================================================================
NOTES
================================================================================

1. Colleague labelling. The four colleagues are shown as Panelist A, B, C, D and
   their model names are never printed in this prompt. The label assignment is
   DETERMINISTIC, not randomised: colleagues appear in the fixed panel order
   (MedGemma-4B, Rnj1-8B, Qwen3-VL-8B, OpenBioLLM-8B, Olmo3-7B) with the
   recipient removed, and letters are assigned by position in that reduced list.
   The mapping is therefore stable for a given recipient across all questions and
   all rounds, and it is recoverable from the panel order alone. Anyone measuring
   positional or identity effects should account for this.

2. Self-reference. The recipient is shown its OWN previous answer in the YOUR
   PREVIOUS POSITION block. This is what makes a debate round "single-turn with
   state". From Round 2 onward the "Your reasoning:" line of that block is empty
   for most recipients, for the same reason the colleague blocks are; see note 4.

3. Visible majority. The running vote tally, including the recipient's own vote,
   is shown to every member. It is a social signal available to the model
   independently of any clinical argument.

4. Empty reasoning lines from Round 2 onward. The "Reasoning:" line of each
   colleague block, and the "Your reasoning:" line of the recipient's own
   previous-position block, are both populated from a clinical_reasoning field.
   At Round 1 the colleagues' Round-0 responses carry that field, so the line is
   full. From Round 2 onward the colleagues' previous responses are debate
   responses, which carry reasoning_update rather than clinical_reasoning, and
   most models did not emit a clinical_reasoning key in the debate format.

   Measured over the colleague blocks actually rendered, that line is empty in
   91.9% of them at Round 2 and 91.8% at Round 3. Only OpenBioLLM-8b (25.8% of
   its debate responses) and Olmo3-7b (9.1%) ever populate the field at all;
   MedGemma-4b, Rnj1-8b and Qwen3-VL-8b never do. The recipient's own
   "Your reasoning:" line is empty at the same rate.

   The argument content visible to a member at Rounds 2 and 3 is therefore
   carried by the "Key Evidence:" lines and the SUMMARY_BLOCK. This is a
   property of the run as executed and is preserved as-is in the transcripts.

5. One template for all three rounds. What differs across rounds is which previous
   position is shown, whether SUMMARY_BLOCK is present, and which round the
   colleague responses are drawn from (always the immediately preceding one).

6. Convergence. At the end of each debate round the members' updated_selected_option
   values are tallied. If the modal option holds at least 3 votes, the council
   returns it. Otherwise the next round fires, up to a maximum of three. See
   "How a question was resolved" in ../README.md.
